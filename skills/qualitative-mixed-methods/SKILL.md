# Qualitative and Mixed Methods

1. Align the question, qualitative tradition, sampling logic, data source, and analytic approach.
2. Document recruitment, researcher position, consent, context, recording, transcription, translation, and security.
3. Define coding, codebook evolution, memoing, negative cases, and team reconciliation.
4. Treat saturation as a justified analytic decision, not a universal sample-size formula.
5. Evaluate credibility, dependability, confirmability, transferability, reflexivity, and auditability.
6. For mixed methods, specify timing, priority, integration point, joint display, and handling of divergent findings.
7. Never fabricate quotations, codes, themes, or consensus.

Return an evidence trail, interpretive risks, reporting checklist, and prioritized revisions.
